What it does while you work

Three things, in the order they matter: it starts without being thought about, it does the client's arithmetic instead of yours, and it does not try to become your invoicing software.

Starting the clock costs two keys

Option+Space

Tempo starts timing the document in front of you and tags it with the client whose folder it sits in: anything under ~/Work/Studio Nord/ is Studio Nord, every time, without a dropdown. The window title goes into the note, so six weeks later the line reads glossary_fixes_v4.docx and not misc. The same two keys stop it.

If the keyboard and trackpad go quiet for 25 minutes, Tempo keeps timing but flags the block as idle and asks when you come back.

Every client rounds differently. Tempo remembers which.

This is the part people are losing money on. A fifteen-minute increment is not a rounding error when it happens eleven times a week. Set the rule once per client and Tempo shows both figures, so you always know what you are giving away and what you are allowed to keep.

Four moments. Nothing in between them is work you have to do.

Questions freelancers actually ask

Does it watch my screen?

It reads the name of the front application and its window title every few seconds and writes them to the local file. No screenshots, no keystrokes, nothing leaves the machine unless you switch sync on. You can exclude applications by name, and most people exclude their bank and their messages.

What if I forget to start the timer at all?

That is what the end-of-day question is for. Tempo keeps a quiet record of what was in front of you in each five minute block, so when the day closes it offers the stretches you never assigned, with a guess attached. The 35 minutes in the band at the top of this page is one of those.

Can I send an invoice from Tempo?

No, and it is not on the list. It writes one line per client and rate and pushes the same lines into Moneybird, FreeAgent or Lexoffice. Invoicing means tax rules in thirty countries, reminders, and dunning letters. That is a different product and other people are better at it.

I already use Toggl. What would change?

Import your CSV export and the old entries keep their clients and notes. What you gain is the rounding rule per client, the end-of-day question and a local database. What you lose is the team view, the report builder and the board. If you work alone, that is a good trade. If you do not, it is not.

What does it run on?

macOS 13 Ventura or later, Windows 11, and Linux as an AppImage tested on Ubuntu and Fedora. The iPhone app starts and stops timers and shows the week; it cannot edit entries yet. There is no browser version, because a timer that dies with a closed tab is not a timer.

What happens if I stop paying?

The app keeps running on the free terms: two active clients, manual entries, CSV export. Sync and the end-of-day question switch off. Nothing is locked, no data is held, and the database stays a file you own.
