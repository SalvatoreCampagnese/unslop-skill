What we do

Three services, usually bought in this order. The first one pays for the other two.

Taking a prototype into production

You have a notebook that gets the answer right most of the time, or a vendor demo that fell over the week it met real data. We build the service around the model: an evaluation set that fails the build when quality drops, a rollback that does not need a deploy, request and response logging you can hand to an auditor, and a runbook your on-call engineer can follow without ringing us. First release is usually eleven weeks from the end of the scoping week.

Bought as a build pod, three months minimum.

Evaluation and acceptance testing

Most of the argument about whether a model is good enough is really an argument about the test. We write the test: a labelled set drawn from your own records, graded by the people who do the job today, with a pass mark agreed in writing before anyone sees a score. You keep the set. It becomes the thing your next supplier has to beat.

Bought as a scoping week, then four to six weeks of build.

Retrieval over documents you already hold

Ingest that respects the permissions on the source system, chunking tuned on your documents rather than on a blog post, answers that cite the page they came from, and a re-index you can run yourself without us. We will also tell you when a plain search index is the better answer, which happens more often than we would like.

Bought as a build pod, usually three months.

Built in-house

Two products we sell, host and support ourselves. The same engineers are on the rota for both.

Ledger

A decision record for automated decisions. Every output is stored with the inputs it saw, the model version, the feature set or prompt, and the name of whoever overrode it. Built for teams who have to explain a decision eighteen months later.

Nine lending and claims teams run it. Charged per decision, from £0.004.

Sift

Document extraction for claims packs, bills of lading and supplier invoices. It reads about 240,000 pages in a normal month across our clients, and sends the three in a hundred it is unsure about to a person, with the reason.

Hosted in Ireland or on your own cluster. From £1,900 a month.

Selected work

Three engagements, each with the number the client used to judge it. All three gave us permission to name them.

Brigg & Marsden

Specialty insurer, Leeds. Client since 2021.

9 hours

first response on a new claim, down from 3.4 days

Claims arrived as email with attachments and were triaged by hand, in the order the inbox happened to show them. We built a triage service that reads the pack, assigns a complexity band and routes it, with a confidence floor below which a handler still reads everything. Just over seven claims in ten are now routed before anyone opens them.

The evaluation set is 4,100 historic claims graded independently by two senior handlers. Where the two disagreed, a third decided, and those cases are kept as a separate harder set. The build fails if agreement with the handlers drops below the mark they set in the second week.

Northgate Freight

Groupage and customs brokerage, Rotterdam.

£410,000

annual keying cost removed, audited by their finance team

Bills of lading and customs paperwork were keyed by six people across two shifts. We extracted the fields that were worth extracting and left alone the ones that were not: four of the eighteen fields on the form still go to a person, because the error cost there is higher than the labour cost.

Two reviewers now handle exceptions instead of six keying everything. Their customs correction rate fell by about a factor of five, which mattered more to the board than the saving did.

Arborfield Mutual

Building society, Reading. Regulated lending.

2 days

median time to an affordability decision, down from eleven

Affordability packs were checked by hand against a policy document that had been amended nine times. We built the document checks, and then the part that turned out to matter: a record of every automated check with the document it read, the clause it applied and the version of the policy in force that day.

Ledger came out of this engagement. Arborfield asked for the audit trail, we built it twice badly, and the third version became a product other lenders now pay for.

How we work

The same shape every time. If an engagement has to depart from it, we say so in writing before you sign.

Days 1 to 5

Scoping week

Two engineers with whoever owns the data and whoever owns the budget, in the same room for at least two of the five days. We read the data, run a cheap baseline, and write a short document: what we think is achievable, what the evaluation set should contain, what it will cost, and whether we think you should do it at all. You pay for the week and keep the document whatever it says.

Weeks 2 to 4

The test comes before the model

Your people grade the evaluation set. A pass mark is written down and signed off by the person who will be accountable for the decisions the system makes. This is the part clients argue about most, and the part that settles every later argument in one sentence.

Weeks 5 to 11

Build, in your repositories

A pod of three works in your version control, your review process and your sprint rhythm. The weekly demo is a run of the harness, not a slide. Your engineers are on the pull requests from the first commit, because a handover to people who have never read the code is not a handover.

After handover

Pairing, then out

A week of pairing with the team that will own it, plus the runbook and the harness. Some clients keep us on call for a quarter or two. Most do not need to after that, and we will tell you when a retainer has stopped earning its fee rather than wait for you to notice.

Questions we get before signing

The six that come up in nearly every second call, answered the way we answer them on the call.

Do you train your own models?

Sometimes. More often we fine-tune a small open-weight model or call a hosted one, and spend the budget on retrieval, evaluation and the failure path instead. Whichever we propose, the reasoning and the cost of the alternative are in the scoping document.

Who owns the code?

You do, from the first commit, in your own repositories. There is no Kestrel licence anywhere in the stack unless you buy Ledger or Sift, and both of those are separable from the rest of the work.

What if it is not good enough?

The pass mark is agreed in week two, so this becomes a question with an answer rather than an argument. If the harness says no at the end of the build, we say no. That has happened twice, and both times we wrote up why and stopped billing.

Can we hire your engineers afterwards?

Yes, and three people have gone that way. The fee is one month of the pod rate if it happens inside six months of handover. We have never objected, because an engineer who wants to own the thing they built is usually right.

What about personal data?

Under a data processing agreement, with a named list of sub-processors and the region you specify. We will not send your records to a hosted model that trains on them, and the providers we propose are named in the scoping document before you sign.

How soon can you start?

A scoping week usually opens up inside three weeks. A build pod needs about six weeks' notice because we do not keep people on the bench. If we cannot staff it properly we will say so rather than hold the slot.

The next hour is with the two engineers who would do the work.

Not a salesperson, and not a deck. Bring the data you are allowed to discuss and the decision you want to automate. If we think the project is wrong, we will say so on that call, which is cheaper for both of us than finding out in week nine.
