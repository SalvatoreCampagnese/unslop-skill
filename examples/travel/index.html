What we add to an advertised fare

Five rules, applied to every result. Figures are medians across the last 90 days of European searches.

Checked baggage
Priced per traveller from the operator’s own table, at the weight you selected, not the cheapest band they publish.
€54
Seat selection
Added only when a party of two or more cannot be seated together without paying. For solo travellers it is left out.
€21
Self-transfer risk
Where two tickets are sold as one journey, we add the cost of the next available fare on the second leg, weighted by how often that connection is missed.
€140
Airport transfer
The published coach or rail fare to the city the search names, for airports more than 35km out. Bergamo, Beauvais, Weeze and eleven others.
€22
Payment fee
What the operator charges for a normal debit or credit card in the country you are booking from.
€8

Questions we get before the first booking

Answered the way we would answer them on the phone.

If you take a flat fee, why would you ever recommend the cheap fare?

We earn the same €4 whichever line you book, so the ranking has nothing to sell. It is also why there is no “sponsored” row and no deal of the week: we have nothing to put in one.

What happens when your total turns out to be wrong?

If you pay more than the figure we showed, we refund the difference up to €60, and we publish how often that happens. It was 3.4% of bookings in February, and the median gap was €14.

Do you book the ticket, or send me to the operator?

You book with the operator, on their site, with their terms. We pass the search across and take the fee separately. If something goes wrong with the journey, your contract is with the people flying the aircraft.

Why is a self-transfer priced so much higher than the ticket?

Because two tickets sold as one journey are two tickets. Miss the first leg and nobody owes you the second. We add the median cost of the next fare on that leg, weighted by how often that connection is actually missed on that route.

Can I search without an account?

Yes, and that is the normal way to use it. An account exists so you can keep a search running and be told when the total moves by more than €15.

Run a search and read the right-hand column.

That column is the whole product. If the totals we show you do not change which fare you would have picked, there is no reason to use Verso, and we would rather you worked that out in one search than after an account and a newsletter.
