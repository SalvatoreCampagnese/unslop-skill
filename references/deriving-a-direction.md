# Deriving a direction

> This file is **vocabulary for naming moves, not a menu to pick from.** If you
> can trace your plan to a line in here, you took it off a list. Start over.

A fix list produces a different template. The only thing that produces a design
is a direction derived from the subject.

## The derivation

1. **Name the subject.** What is it, who uses it, when, and in what state of
   mind? "A freelance invoicing tool used on Sunday night by someone who hates
   admin" is a brief. "A SaaS landing page" is not.
2. **Find its material world.** What does the subject's actual world look like,
   physically? Not its competitors' websites — its world.
   - payroll → forms, ledgers, carbon copies, rubber stamps, column rules
   - climbing → topo maps, chalk, anodised aluminium, contour lines
   - a pasta shop → brown paper, flour dust, brass, hand-lettering
   - developer tooling → terminals, monospace, diffs, exit codes, log output
   - a clinic → signage, legibility at distance, calm, lots of white space
3. **Take colour, type, texture and density from there.** A ledger is not
   "cream and terracotta" — it is off-white paper, grey-blue rule lines, one red
   for corrections. That is four colours with four jobs, derived.
4. **Write the plan in five lines** — see SKILL.md step 5.
5. **State the provenance of every choice.** A choice with no source in step 2
   is a choice from a list.

## Naming moves (vocabulary only)

Density · measure · rhythm · hierarchy · contrast ratio · one signature detail ·
restraint · asymmetry · grid tension · figure-ground · texture · weight ·
optical size · ink coverage · rule lines · generous margins · tight leading ·
oversized type as image · flat ground · earned decoration.

Use these words to describe what you did and why. Do not use them as options.

## The second-order trap

Models absorbed the anti-slop advice, so the "tasteful" escape routes are now
defaults of their own. Known coordinates to avoid landing on by accident:

- cream `#F4F1EA` + terracotta `#D97757` + high-contrast serif (SD1)
- near-black `#0B0B0B` + one acid-green or vermilion accent (SD2)
- emerald `#10b981` as the accent chosen because it is "not purple" (SD3)
- mono micro-labels + `A · B · C` meta strings + `→` on every link (SD4)
- newspaper rules and zero radius on a product page (SD5)
- orange accent + serif, the 2026 "I avoided purple" signature (SD6)

Avoiding these is not the goal either. Deriving is the goal. If the subject's
world genuinely is brown paper and brass, cream and terracotta is *correct* —
state where it came from, and the gate is satisfied.

Run `--record=<name>` on every pass so SD0 can catch you repeating yourself.
Your own last three passes are a style menu too.

## Voice, for text mode

The same logic applies to prose. Derive the register from who is writing and to
whom, then write it down in `VOICE.md`:

- who is speaking, and what they are allowed to claim
- what they would never say
- sentence-length range, and whether fragments are allowed
- how much hedging the subject honestly requires
- the words this document owns, and the words it refuses

Then cut machine habits against that contract — not against an average of good
writing, which is how you get a different default.

## When to stop

If the original is already strong, make the few cuts it needs and stop. A clean
audit is a valid result. Over-designing to prove the pass happened is its own
kind of slop.
